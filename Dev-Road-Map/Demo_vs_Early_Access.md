# Demo vs. Early Access

**What this is:** a short standalone reference distinguishing the free Demo from the paid Early Access
release, split out into its own file so it's easy to find on its own — the same material also lives as
Section 5 of `Early_Access_vs_Launch_Content_Split.md`, which covers the fuller Early Access content-split
plan this file assumes. Written 2026-07-23.

---

## The two things, side by side

- **Price.** Demo is free, always. Early Access is paid — the actual revenue mechanism funding the three
  items in that file's Category 1 (voice acting, animation, music).
- **Scope and lifespan.** A Demo is a **fixed, curated slice** — a short vertical slice (most likely the
  opening sequence: climb the CN Tower, reactivate the radio, hit the false-victory beat, per `Reference/
  Main-Storyline-Candidates.md`) frozen at a specific polish level, that doesn't grow or change based on
  feedback. Early Access is a **genuine, evolving subset of the real game** — the actual companion roster,
  romance arcs, and content described in that file's Categories 1-3, expected to expand over months as
  development continues toward 1.0.
- **Saves/progress.** Demo saves generally do **not** carry into the full game (Steam treats it as a separate
  depot tied to the main store page). Early Access saves **do** carry forward into Launch — same app, same
  depot, just an earlier point in its life.
- **Feedback loop.** Early Access explicitly invites player feedback as a real development input. A Demo's
  purpose is conversion, not iteration — it isn't rebuilt in response to what players say about it.
- **Purpose.** The Demo's job is building wishlist velocity (see `Steam_Launch_Strategy_and_AI_Disclosure.md`
  Section 2) and getting into Steam Next Fest. Early Access's job is converting that wishlist momentum into
  actual sales that fund the rest of production.

## How this chains together for Toronto Fell Out specifically

The Demo comes *first*, chronologically — a small, free taste covering the CN Tower climb through the
false-victory reveal, with no companion romance necessarily included, timed around a Steam Next Fest. This
is a natural, already-true parallel to how Goodsprings functioned for *Fallout: New Vegas*: a compact,
self-contained opening sequence that's also the first thing every player experiences, doubling as the
Demo's own vertical slice (see `Demo_Content_Specification.md` Section 6 for the fuller argument). Early
Access follows, as the actual paid product described throughout `Early_Access_vs_Launch_Content_Split.md` —
expanding outward from the tower sequence once more of the game (companions, districts, the resolved
false-victory direction) actually exists to expand into.
