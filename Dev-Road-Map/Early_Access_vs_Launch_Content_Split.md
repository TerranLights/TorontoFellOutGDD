# Early Access vs. Launch — Content Split

**What this is:** a working answer to a release-strategy question, not a GDD design document — what can feasibly be justified as exclusive to the full "Launch" release, held back from Early Access.

**The plan this answers:** release Toronto Fell Out in Early Access first — partly to raise funds for hiring professional 3D animators, voice actors, and bands/musicians — then later release the actual, complete, proper game (not Early Access). DLCs wait until after that full launch; they are not part of this question at all, since they're already understood to come later. Toronto Fell Out has no DLC plan yet, so nothing below assumes any specific DLC content or count.

---

## 1. The three things funding is directly for

These are the cleanest, most easily justified Launch-exclusive items, because they map directly onto what the Early Access money is actually raised to pay for:

- **Voice acting.** Early Access ships text-only, or with a companion or two temp-voiced as a taste of what's coming. Full voice acting for every companion and major NPC arrives at launch.
- **3D animation polish.** Early Access ships with functional but simpler rigging/mocap; launch brings the professional animation pass, especially on companion romance scenes and combat.
- **Original music.** Early Access ships with stock/royalty-free or placeholder tracks; launch brings in actual composers/bands for the game's various musical textures. Toronto Fell Out has no companions, districts, or musical identities designed yet, so there's nothing to enumerate here beyond the category itself — "hear the real soundtrack" is a tangible, legible launch feature in its own right, not just an internal production milestone, whatever that soundtrack ends up being.

---

## 2. Content completeness, not just polish

- **Full companion roster** — a category concept, not a specific split. Early Access could ship with a genuine subset — whichever companions are most fully designed at the time of release — while the remaining companions complete during the Early Access development window and land at 1.0. This is the single most common Early Access pattern in the industry (Baldur's Gate 3 shipped Act 1 only during EA; Hades and Slay the Spire both grew their full cast and content during their own EA periods). **Toronto Fell Out has zero designed companions right now**, so which specific companions would fall on which side of this split cannot be answered yet — this is a category to plan for, not a roster to divide.
- **Post-romance mini-questlines and Significant Object rewards** (`Game-Mechanics/Core-Mechanics/Companion-System.md`'s Post-Romance Mini-Questline Reward section). Easy to stage once companions exist: base romance arcs ship in Early Access, the deeper post-romance layer arrives as a launch addition.
- **Full companion home designs** (`Worldspace/Locations-and-Levels/Romance_Unlocked_Homes.md`). Interiors could ship simplified during Early Access, fully realized at launch — again, a category to plan for once actual companion homes are designed.
- **Endgame/completionist content** — New Game+, achievements, a final full perk-cycle balance pass.

### Romance content itself: a category-level decision, not a roster

The underlying question this section answers for a companion-driven RPG is whether romance content ships in Early Access at all, or waits for Launch. The industry precedent (Baldur's Gate 3's own Early Access, where romance content was a major part of what got people talking) argues strongly for including a genuine subset of full romance arcs in Early Access rather than holding romance back entirely — it's one of the biggest draws and word-of-mouth generators in this genre, and hiding it entirely risks hiding the thing most likely to win over the exact audience a funding drive depends on.

**As a category, not a roster:** a small subset of companions — full Gate 1/Gate 2 romance design, signal lines, and romance beats per `Companion-System.md` — ship in Early Access at whatever voice/animation/music polish level the rest of Early Access ships at (per Category 1 above). The full companion roster, and any post-romance mini-questlines, are what's held back for Launch. **Which specific companions land in that Early Access subset is not decided, and cannot be decided, because no companions exist yet.** Don't invent a shortlist here — when a real roster exists, it should be selected against criteria like: personality variety (avoid two near-identical archetypes both in the EA subset), critical-path visibility (companions a typical playthrough is likely to actually meet), and how far along each character's own design is at the time the cut has to be made.

## 3. Things that are genuinely hard to do well before the game is finished

- **Full localization** (see `Dev-Road-Map/Localization_Language_List.md`'s own Tier 1 list). Translating a game that's still actively changing wastes translator budget on content that will be rewritten; this is standard industry practice as a launch-only item, not a corner being cut.
- **Final balance pass** across leveling, perks, MAPLE-BUD stats, and combat difficulty — generally wants the full content set in players' hands before the numbers get locked down.
- **Accessibility features** (subtitle timing, colorblind modes, control remapping) — usually a late-stage polish pass once the UI itself has stabilized.

---

## 4. Marketing-legible "this is the real release" signals

Steam achievements and trading cards, a proper finalized opening cinematic, finished box art/key art — all cheap to justify, and useful for the "this is now a finished product" messaging that separates a full launch from Early Access in players' own minds, distinct from the underlying content questions above. A photo mode is a common example of this category in the genre generally: it's only genuinely worth shipping once character models, animations, and environments are past the "functional but simpler" Early Access baseline described under Category 1, and it pairs naturally with the other items in this category (finalized key art, a polished opening cinematic) as a "this is the real release" signal in its own right. Whether Toronto Fell Out actually includes a photo mode, and if so at which stage, isn't decided — it's listed here only as an example of the category.

## Recommendation

Lead marketing and backer messaging with Category 1 specifically (voice, animation, music) — it's the most emotionally legible pitch to an Early Access audience, since it maps directly onto what their money is actually funding. Categories 2 and 3 are normal, well-precedented industry practice and won't read as a bait-and-switch to players, but they're secondary to the direct funding story Category 1 tells on its own.

**One refinement to keep in mind when a real companion roster eventually exists:** don't let the Category 1 polish-deferral logic accidentally justify deferring romance content itself. A small subset of companions' romance arcs should ship in Early Access at whatever polish level the rest of the game ships at; only the *remaining* companions and their romance content are deferred to Launch. See the category-level note under Section 2, above — this is a shape to apply once companions are designed, not a decision already made about which ones.
