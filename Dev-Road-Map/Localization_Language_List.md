# Localization — Target Language List

**Status:** Draft. Needs budget/scope confirmation before treated as final — see Open Questions at the
bottom.

---

## Approach

Two factors went into this list: **market ROI** (standard industry-priority languages for a Steam-first
CRPG — Simplified Chinese, Spanish, Brazilian Portuguese, German, French, Russian, Japanese, Korean, and
Polish are all standard high-ROI Steam localization targets industry-wide) and **thematic resonance** —
but where Inner Tepenia's own version of this file drew thematic resonance from a fictional census of an
invented Antarctic city, Toronto Fell Out gets to draw on something better: **Toronto is a real city with
genuinely, famously enormous linguistic diversity**, and several of its real neighborhoods are themselves
well-known cultural landmarks. Where standard Steam ROI and a real, visible Toronto community point the same
direction, that language is an easy priority call — several do, which is the happy accident of setting a
game in one of the most diverse cities on Earth.

Full simultaneous localization into every tier below is unlikely to be realistic for a solo dev at launch —
this list is meant to be worked top-down, not all-at-once. See Open Questions. Note also: this file
describes real, well-documented Toronto neighborhoods and communities as general background context for a
*prioritization* decision — it is not a claim about which specific characters or story content the game
will feature, and nothing here should be read as inventing new in-game districts, factions, or characters.

---

## Tier 1 — Launch Priority

High Steam market share, strong PC/CRPG purchasing patterns, and/or a genuinely large, visible real-world
Toronto community tied to the language.

| Language | Why |
|---|---|
| **English** | Base/source language |
| **Simplified Chinese** | Largest Steam player base by most estimates; standard top priority for any Steam release. Also a straightforward thematic double-hit: Chinese-Canadians are one of the largest and most visible communities in Toronto proper, with multiple real Chinatowns (downtown at Spadina/Dundas, "East Chinatown" along Gerrard/Broadview) and a large Chinese-Canadian population across Scarborough and the wider GTA. |
| **French** | Standard ROI aside, this is close to a mandatory pick for a *Canadian*-set game specifically: French is Canada's other official language, Quebec is a large PC/Steam market in its own right, and Ontario has a genuine Franco-Ontarian population. A Toronto-set game skipping French reads as a notable omission in a way that wouldn't apply to most other Steam titles. |
| **Brazilian Portuguese** | One of the fastest-growing Steam markets on its own ROI merits. Also a real thematic tie: Toronto's Little Portugal (Dundas St. West around Ossington/Dovercourt) is a historically deep, still-visible Portuguese-Canadian community dating to mid-20th-century immigration waves. |
| **Italian** | Toronto has one of the largest Italian-Canadian populations of any city outside Italy, expressed in two distinct, well-known neighborhoods — Little Italy (College St.) and Corso Italia (St. Clair Ave. West) — plus deep civic presence (festivals, business associations) beyond those two strips. Steam market size is moderate rather than top-tier on ROI alone, but the local thematic weight is unusually strong for a game actually set in this city. |
| **Korean** | A strong existing PC/RPG-genre fanbase in Korea on ROI grounds alone, paired with a genuine, well-known Toronto neighborhood identity: Koreatown along Bloor St. West (roughly Bathurst to Christie) is one of the city's most recognizable ethnic commercial strips. |
| **Spanish** | Large global Steam market and a standard top-tier localization target regardless of Toronto-specific community size; Toronto and the wider GTA also have a real and growing Latin American population. |
| **German** | Large, high-purchasing-power PC/Steam market — a standard industry pick independent of any Toronto-specific tie. (Toronto's own German-Canadian community is modest by comparison to nearby Southwestern Ontario, e.g. Kitchener-Waterloo's own German heritage — worth noting honestly rather than manufacturing a stronger local tie than actually exists.) |
| **Russian** | Large PC/Steam market with strong CRPG-genre affinity historically; Toronto has a real, if more modest, Russian-speaking community (parts of North York) than the languages above. |

## Tier 2 — Strong Secondary Candidates

Large-enough Steam markets, or thematic ties strong enough to prioritize once Tier 1 is covered — including
several genuinely large Toronto communities that simply aren't standard AAA/indie Steam localization
targets on ROI grounds alone.

| Language | Why |
|---|---|
| **Japanese** | Strong RPG-genre audience on ROI grounds — this genre sells disproportionately well in Japan. Toronto's own Japanese-Canadian community is smaller today than the other Tier 1/2 communities (the historic pre-WWII "Japantown" around Spadina/Dundas was dispersed following wartime internment and never fully reconstituted at the same scale), so the case here leans on genre ROI more than present-day local population size. |
| **Traditional Chinese** | Distinct localization from Simplified Chinese, targeting Taiwan/Hong Kong markets specifically — standard practice for any release already doing Simplified Chinese. |
| **Tagalog (Filipino)** | Toronto and the wider GTA have one of the largest Filipino-Canadian populations in the country, dispersed across the city rather than anchored to one single historic strip. Not a standard high-ROI Steam localization target in the industry generally, but genuinely worth flagging given local population size. |
| **Greek** | Greektown along the Danforth is one of Toronto's most recognizable neighborhood identities (home to the annual Taste of the Danforth festival) and reflects a real, long-established Greek-Canadian community. Steam market size on its own is small. |
| **Polish** | Smaller Steam market by raw size, but CRPG-genre players are disproportionately represented there (CD Projekt's home market) — good ROI-per-capita for this specific genre. Also a real Toronto tie: Roncesvalles Village is popularly known as "Little Poland," home to a long-established Polish-Canadian community. |
| **Punjabi / Hindi / Urdu** | The Gerrard India Bazaar ("Little India," along Gerrard St. East) is one of the largest South Asian marketplace districts in North America, and South Asian communities (Punjabi, Tamil, Gujarati, and others) are among the fastest-growing populations across Toronto and the broader GTA (notably Scarborough, Brampton, and Mississauga). Not a standard Steam ROI target on its own, but a genuinely large and fast-growing real population. |
| **Tamil** | Scarborough is widely cited as home to one of the largest Tamil populations outside South Asia itself — a genuinely notable real-world demographic fact for a Toronto-set game, even though it isn't a standard Steam localization market. |

## Tier 3 — Thematically Notable, Smaller Markets

Not standard high-ROI localization targets on their own, but worth flagging given how much Toronto's real
demographic makeup includes these communities. Realistic candidates for post-launch or community
localization rather than day-one.

| Language | Why |
|---|---|
| **Vietnamese** | A real, historically established Vietnamese-Canadian community in Toronto, with a visible presence in and around the city's Chinatown corridors and parts of Scarborough. |
| **Ukrainian** | Bloor West Village (near High Park) has a long-established Ukrainian-Canadian community and civic identity (churches, cultural institutions, the annual Bloor West Village Toronto Ukrainian Festival), further reinforced by a more recent refugee-driven wave of arrivals. Ukrainian also has some standalone Steam market/ROI case independent of the Toronto tie specifically. |
| **Somali** | Etobicoke, particularly the Dixon Road corridor, is home to one of Canada's largest Somali-Canadian communities. Essentially unprecedented as a Steam localization target — no real industry precedent to point to — but genuinely notable given the real population, and worth flagging as an experimental, community-driven candidate rather than a professional day-one target. |
| **Persian (Farsi)** | North York has a large Iranian-Canadian population, informally reflected in the area's own colloquial "Tehranto" nickname. No real Steam localization precedent, but a genuinely large local community. |
| **Turkish** | A common indie/mid-size Steam localization target independent of thematic ties, plus a regional-pricing market consideration; Toronto's own Turkish-Canadian community is smaller than the communities above. |

## Open Questions

- **Realistic launch scope for a solo dev.** Full Tier 1 (9 languages including English) is a substantial
  localization budget/effort even before voice acting is considered. Needs an honest scope decision: full
  text-only localization for Tier 1, a smaller subset, or English-only (plus French, given the Canadian-
  setting case above) at launch with post-launch expansion?
- **Voice acting scope**, if any — localization cost is dramatically different for text-only vs. full VO,
  and affects which languages are realistic at all.
- **Community/fan translation policy** — many indie CRPGs (including genre touchstones like the classic
  Fallout games) rely partly on community-driven translation for lower-tier languages, which is especially
  relevant for Tier 2/3 languages here (Tagalog, Punjabi/Hindi/Urdu, Tamil, Somali, Persian) that have large
  real local populations but no standard industry localization precedent to lean on. Worth deciding whether
  Toronto Fell Out supports that officially (tooling, moddable string files) even if it can't fund
  professional localization for every language on this list.
- **Priority order within Tier 1** if not all 9 (including English) can happen simultaneously — French's case
  is arguably strengthened, relative to a generic Steam ROI ranking, by the Canadian-setting argument above,
  which is worth weighing against the raw market-size ordering of the other Tier 1 languages.
