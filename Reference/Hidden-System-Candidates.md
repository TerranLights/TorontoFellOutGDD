# Hidden System Candidates (Karma Replacement)

Toronto Fell Out will **not** use Fallout's "Karma" system by name. This file holds candidate concepts for a hidden, non-visible tracked system that takes on some of Karma's structural role (a background meter shaped by player choices, invisible on the HUD, that quietly reshapes NPC reactions and gates late-game rewards) without actually being Karma, and without being Fallout: DUST's "Sanity" system either. Each candidate below is meant to be thematically native to Toronto Fell Out specifically, not a reskin of either source.

**Why this matters for existing canon**: `player mechanics/Perks.md`'s Level 50 perks (Ain't Like That Now, Just Lucky I'm Alive, Thought You Died) currently branch on Karma thresholds, ported directly from Fallout: New Vegas. Once one of these candidates is chosen, that trio needs renaming and re-theming to match — draft replacement names are sketched under each candidate below, but none of this is finalized.

## 1. Cohesion *(current recommendation)*

**Concept**: A hidden meter tracking whether the player's actions help Toronto's fractured survivor communities cohere — rebuild trust, cooperation, shared infrastructure, reconciliation between people and factions — or deepen the fracture: exploitation, isolation, distrust, chaos.

**Why it fits this game specifically**: it's a direct callback to the title itself. Toronto *fell out* — fractured, as in a falling-out between people, not just a nuclear "fallout." Cohesion measures whether the player is the one putting the city back together or the one finishing the job of tearing it apart. No other game could use this name and have it mean the same thing.

**What moves it**:
- Quest resolutions that unite vs. divide factions or communities
- Choices to share vs. hoard scarce resources (food, medicine, fuel, shelter)
- Whether settlements/outposts the player builds serve a wider community or just the player
- Whether companion personal arcs resolve toward reconciliation or estrangement
- Willingness to broker peace vs. exploit conflict between factions for personal gain

**Mechanical hooks**:
- NPC dialogue/reaction shifts, parallel to how Karma shifts NPC greetings in Fallout
- Possible gating of a late-game "the city actually rebuilds" vs. "the city stays fractured forever" ending state
- Level 50 perk trio (draft names, not final):
  - Low Cohesion → **Scorched Earth** — fully embracing the role of an agent of fracture; combat/speed bonuses reflecting having given up on holding anything together
  - Neutral → **Between the Cracks** — a scrappy survivor who's neither uniter nor destroyer; a near-death emergency bonus
  - High Cohesion → **Someone to Come Home To** — reflects having built something people rally around; a bonus tied to community goodwill

## 2. Frost / Thaw

**Concept**: Tracks emotional/moral temperature — how cold (ruthless, self-preserving, willing to let others freeze or go without) vs. warm (generous, protective, willing to share scarce resources at personal cost) the player has become, framed through Toronto's own radioactive-winter motif already established in the README ("Toronto's legendary winter storms now come with radioactive snow").

**Why it fits this game specifically**: ties directly into the setting's signature environmental hazard rather than an abstract morality axis — the same cold that's slowly killing the city is also the metaphor for what it does to the people in it.

**What moves it**:
- Choices to share vs. withhold food, medicine, shelter, warmth with others
- Treatment of the vulnerable (elderly, children, injured companions, strangers caught in a storm) during resource scarcity
- Willingness to leave people behind vs. risk yourself for others

**Mechanical hooks**:
- Could double-dip mechanically with actual cold/radiation-resistance systems, not just flavor
- Level 50 perk trio (draft names, not final):
  - Deep Frost → **Dead of Winter** — icy, efficient, merciless combat bonuses
  - Neutral → **Surviving the Season** — a plain, unglamorous survivor's bonus
  - Deep Thaw → **First Thaw** — a warmth/regeneration-themed bonus

## 3. Grace

**Concept**: A wry, dark-comedy-flavored meter riffing on the Canadian-politeness stereotype (the "sorry" culture joke already present in the game's tone) — tracks courteous, fair, community-minded conduct vs. selfish or gratuitously cruel conduct. Played completely straight mechanically even though the flavor is a joke, matching the README's own "dark humor... darkly humorous" framing.

**Why it fits this game specifically**: leans hardest into the game's specific comedic identity (companions with "strong opinions about poutine and survival," Canadian-flavored dark humor) rather than the setting's environment or its title.

**What moves it**:
- Whether the player extends basic courtesy under pressure (queueing, thanking, apologizing) even in a lawless setting
- Fair dealing in trades vs. price-gouging or theft
- Treatment of service-worker-type NPCs specifically (a returning comedic throughline given the "Former TTC Driver" background flavor already in the README)
- Restraint in violence when it isn't necessary vs. gratuitous cruelty

**Mechanical hooks**:
- Comedic dialogue variation is the strongest natural fit here — NPCs commenting on the player's manners (or lack thereof) even mid-crisis
- Discounts/friendliness from NPCs scale with it
- Level 50 perk trio (draft names, not final):
  - Low Grace → **Eh, Whatever** — a sardonic, careless-but-effective bonus
  - Neutral → **Just Getting By**
  - High Grace → **Sorry, Eh?** — ironically named for maximum politeness

## 4. Static

**Concept**: Frames the hidden meter as literal signal interference/noise the player generates across whatever remains of Toronto's radio, rumor, and surveillance networks. Chaotic, violent, or attention-drawing play raises Static (the world gets more hostile and paranoid around the player, harder to reach quiet/peaceful outcomes); careful, quiet, principled play keeps it low (the world trusts or ignores the player more, easier access to peaceful resolutions).

**Why it fits this game specifically**: more systemic/impersonal than a morality axis — it's not "are you good or evil," it's "how much noise are you making in a world that's trying to stay quiet and rebuild." Could tie into an actual in-world communications network (radio stations, dispatch chatter, rumor networks) as a diegetic justification, similar to how Inner Tepenia's "Arcanet" ties its own network into gameplay.

**What moves it**:
- Collateral damage and public violence
- Robbing/disrupting radio stations or communication infrastructure
- Drawing overt factional attention vs. operating under the radar
- Discretion in resolving conflicts quietly vs. loudly

**Mechanical hooks**:
- Could gate access to certain quiet/diplomatic solutions the same way high Infamy blocks peaceful options in Fallout: New Vegas
- Level 50 perk trio (draft names, not final):
  - High Static → **Dead Air** — an ironic bonus for fully embracing being loud and chaotic
  - Neutral → **On the Wire**
  - Low Static → **Radio Silence**
