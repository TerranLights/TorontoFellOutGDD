# End-Screen Presentation Concept — Format Proposal

**Status:** 🟡 concept/format proposal, not yet fleshed out and not tied to any confirmed in-world technology. Adapted from Inner Tepenia's own `End_Screen_Presentation_Concept.md`, with all IT-specific set dressing (its holographic-hologram tech, its specific epilogue location) stripped out. Complements `Main-Endings/Climax_Structure_and_District_Ending_Consequences.md`, which already anticipates its own per-location "Minor Negatives" lists (once populated) being delivered as epilogue material — this document is a proposed answer to how that delivery could actually work, not a locked decision.

---

## The Core Idea

Fallout: New Vegas closes with a series of 2D slideshow images, one per faction/location/character thread, each narrated in voiceover describing the resulting fate based on the player's choices.

The proposal here keeps the same *function* (a sequence of vignettes covering the game's various threads, shaped by player choice) while considering a different *presentation*: instead of flat 2D images projected onto a screen, each vignette could be a fuller, more dimensional scene — closer to a lived-in tableau than a static photograph. **This is explicitly a presentation-format idea, not an assumption that Toronto Fell Out has any particular epilogue-projection technology already established.** Unlike Inner Tepenia (which could justify a holographic epilogue diegetically, since holographic tech is already established elsewhere in that setting), Toronto Fell Out has made no such commitment — there is no in-world tech confirmed to exist that would explain *how* the player is shown this sequence. That's flagged as an open question below, not assumed away.

## The Asset-Variable Mechanic

The part of this concept worth keeping regardless of presentation format: each vignette can encode information two ways at once.

1. **Character/thread-level narration** — the broad, general outcome for a given person, location, or faction, delivered the way FNV's own slides are (voiceover, text, or an in-fiction equivalent describing what became of them).
2. **Physical asset variables, shown rather than told** — small material details within the scene that silently reflect specific player-driven state, without needing their own line of narration. The example carried over from Inner Tepenia's own capture of this idea: whether a specific door is open or closed. A detail like this can carry real informational weight (was this place abandoned in a hurry, is it still in use, is someone home) purely through set dressing, the way a scene's staging can imply things a script never says outright.

**Why this matters mechanically:** it lets the epilogue system account for a much wider range of granular, player-specific outcomes than narration alone could carry without becoming bloated or repetitive. `Climax_Structure_and_District_Ending_Consequences.md`'s own per-location "Minor Negatives" lists, once populated, are exactly the kind of granular, specific consequence (a status change, a relationship shift, a piece of infrastructure's new condition) that could be conveyed as an asset variable inside a vignette rather than requiring its own dedicated narrated slide.

**Example, illustrative only (no real content invented):** once Toronto Fell Out has an actual location with a Victory Condition and Minor Negatives written up, an epilogue vignette for that location could narrate, broadly, what became of its population under that victory — while a background asset variable (a door left open or closed, a light on or off, a sign repaired or still broken) silently reflects some more granular outcome already established in that location's own Minor Negatives list, without needing to be spoken aloud at all.

## Open for Future Development

- **The diegetic-justification question is genuinely open, and is the single biggest difference from Inner Tepenia's version of this concept.** Inner Tepenia could hang its epilogue presentation on already-established holographic tech (Leo's holographic performer posters, Gemini's holographic data displays). Toronto Fell Out has no equivalent in-world technology confirmed yet. Before committing to any particular presentation (holographic, purely cinematic, an in-world screen/recording of some kind, or left unexplained/abstracted the way FNV's own slides never explain their own presentation), this needs an answer — or a deliberate decision to leave it unexplained, the way FNV itself does.
- Which specific outcomes get full narrated treatment vs. which get folded into background asset variables is not yet mapped for any location, since no locations exist yet to map.
- Whether every eventual location/faction ending gets its own full vignette, or whether some are folded together, is not yet decided — this depends on `Climax_Structure_and_District_Ending_Consequences.md`'s own per-location Victory Condition / Main Negative / Minor Negatives template being populated first.
