# Act 1 — The Climb: Beat Structure

**Status:** the only act with an actual beat-by-beat breakdown, because it's the only act built on content Toronto Fell Out has actually designed — the CN Tower climb and its false-victory reveal, per `Reference/Main-Storyline-Candidates.md`. Adapted structurally from Inner Tepenia's own hook-progression documents (`Main_Story-Hook_Progression.md`, `Act2_Story_Progression.md`), which organized district-to-district travel around NPC hooks, environmental hooks, and an explicit "reason not to go to the final hub yet" per beat. Toronto Fell Out's Act 1 isn't a hub-and-spoke structure — it's a single vertical climb — so that template is adapted rather than copied: beats here are floors/zones of the ascent rather than districts, and hooks are what pulls the player forward and upward rather than sideways to a different location.

**No specific NPCs, factions, or named locations below.** Toronto Fell Out has none designed yet. Where Inner Tepenia's template called for a named NPC or district, this document uses a generic role placeholder (marked TBD) instead.

---

## Beat 1: The Approach

**Location:** Ground level, working inward/upward toward the CN Tower's base.

**Goal:** Establish why reaching and reactivating the transmitter looks both achievable and worth doing, before the player has committed to the climb.

**Hooks (illustrative slots, no specific content designed yet):**
- A [TBD — survivor, settlement contact, or found recording] frames the tower's transmitter as the closest thing to a plan anyone in the area has: if it's broadcasting, someone out there might finally know Toronto is still alive.
- Environmental storytelling establishes the tower as a landmark everyone can see and nobody has reached — previous attempts visibly failed (wreckage, abandoned gear, a marked-up but incomplete route) without needing dialogue to say so.
- The radioactive-winter setting itself supplies urgency: whatever the player is climbing toward, staying at ground level indefinitely is not a stable long-term option.

**Outcome:** the player commits to the climb, with base equipment and a plausible in-fiction reason (rescue, answers, or simply "something has to change").

## Beat 2: The Ascent

**Location:** Interior of the CN Tower, multiple floors/zones.

**Goal:** A multi-stage vertical dungeon that gives the climb texture rather than being a single loading corridor — environmental storytelling, optional side content, and the first taste of what's actually waiting at the top.

**Structural notes:**
- This is the natural home for `Game-Mechanics/Character-Creation/Skills.md`'s **Acrobatics** skill (climbing, balance, vaulting, fall-damage reduction) — explicitly called out in that document as "notably relevant given Toronto's vertical ruins (CN Tower, PATH tunnels, derelict towers)." The ascent should be built to make that relevance concrete: multiple viable routes up (climbing, structural shortcuts, more conventional stairwell/elevator remnants), consistent with `General-Overview-Notes/Design-Philosophy.md`'s **Minimum Five Solutions** rule for non-story-gated obstacles.
- **First foreshadowing of Option 2 (the awakened automated defense network):** per `Main-Storyline-Candidates.md`, the tower's full-power broadcast is the trigger signal a dormant, non-sentient automated system was built to wait for. The ascent is the natural place to seed that this system exists and is currently inert — a dormant Securitron-/Sentry-Bot-style unit glimpsed but not yet active, environmental logs referencing "standing orders" or "cleared for anomalies," damage patterns suggesting the tower was defended once and isn't anymore. Nothing here should activate the system yet — that's the false-victory's second consequence, arriving all at once at the top, not leaking out early.
- **Optional exploration and side content** slots in here freely — this is a large, vertical, semi-ruined structure, and nothing about the critical path requires the player to see all of it.

**Outcome:** the player reaches the transmitter room with a sense of the tower's scale and history, and at least one planted, not-yet-paid-off thread pointing toward Option 2.

---

## Beat 3: The False Victory

**Location:** The transmitter room, at the top of the CN Tower.

**Goal:** The single most important beat in the entire main quest — the moment the player believes they've won, immediately followed by the reveal that they haven't.

**Structure, per `Main-Storyline-Candidates.md`'s combined structure:**
1. The player reactivates the transmitter. Played straight — this should feel like a genuine, satisfying triumph in the moment, not undercut in advance by tone or dialogue. The false victory only works if it's allowed to actually feel like victory first.
2. **The reveal hits as three near-simultaneous consequences**, not one:
   - The signal reaches the outside world — setting up **Option 1** (the quarantine reveal) as the slow-burn political mystery that runs underneath the rest of the game.
   - The signal is the exact trigger the tower's dormant automated custodian system (seeded in Beat 2) was built to wait for — setting up **Option 2** (the awakened defense network) as an immediate, escalating mechanical threat.
   - The signal collides with or overrides an existing, weaker signal already quietly in use — setting up **Option 4** (the frequency-squatting faction) as the first concrete, personal-scale antagonist the player actually meets.
3. **Option 3 (the buried asset) is not revealed here.** Per `Main-Storyline-Candidates.md`, it's the unifying secret behind all three of the above, revealed gradually across the whole game rather than dumped in this one scene. Beat 3 plants the *effects* of the buried asset's existence (the quarantine, the custodian system's aggression, the squatting faction's motive) without naming the cause yet.

**Outcome:** the player leaves the transmitter room not with a victory screen, but with three live problems where they expected zero. This is the pivot into Act 2.
