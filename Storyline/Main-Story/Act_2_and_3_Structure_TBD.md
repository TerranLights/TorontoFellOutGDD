# Acts 2 & 3 — Structure (TBD, Template Ready)

**Status:** structural principle only, populated as far as `Reference/Main-Storyline-Candidates.md` currently supports and no further. Toronto Fell Out has zero designed districts, factions, or NPCs — this document does not invent any to fill the gap. What follows is the shape Acts 2 and 3 should take once that content exists, adapted from Inner Tepenia's own Act 2/Act 3 documents (`Act2_Story_Progression.md`'s district-to-district hook progression, `Act-3_and_Climax_-_Final_Confrontation_Ideas_and_Possibilities.md`'s climax-scenario brainstorm), with all district-specific and faction-specific content stripped out.

## Act 2 — Three Threads

Per the combined structure in `Main-Storyline-Candidates.md`, the false victory at the end of Act 1 triggers three consequences that develop in parallel through Act 2:

1. **The political mystery (Option 1 — the quarantine).** A slow-burn thread investigating who's enforcing the quarantine, why Toronto specifically, and what it would take to break, expose, or negotiate with it. This thread doesn't require the player to be anywhere in particular at any given time — it's driven by investigation and revelation rather than a physical threat requiring immediate response.
2. **The mechanical threat (Option 2 — the awakened defense network).** An active, escalating, "field-by-field" threat — the custodian system doesn't have to wake up everywhere at once, and can activate incrementally across whatever locations Toronto Fell Out eventually designs, giving natural pacing and stakes-escalation across the map. This is the thread with the most inherent urgency and the best fit for forced-cooperation scenarios (factions who otherwise wouldn't work together, dealing with a shared mechanical problem).
3. **The personal antagonist (Option 4 — the frequency-squatting faction).** The fastest-paying-off thread, since it can be introduced almost immediately (someone reacting, furious or scared, to having lost exclusive control of the airwaves) rather than requiring slow-burn worldbuilding. The natural home for the game's first investigative "who were they talking to, and why does it matter" thread.

**Design principle (non-linear, per Inner Tepenia's own Act 2 practice):** the player should be able to engage these three threads in any order, ignore any of them for long stretches, or pursue only one exclusively. None should hard-gate progress on the others except where a specific story beat genuinely requires it. Once real content exists, this is the place to write actual hooks — NPC-offered leads, environmental discoveries, faction requests — that pull the player from one thread into another, the way Inner Tepenia's own Act 2 document wrote 3–5 hooks per district transition. That level of specificity can't be written yet because there's nothing (no districts, no NPCs, no factions) to hang the hooks on.

**Not yet designed, and shouldn't be guessed at:**
- Where these three threads are physically set (blocked on districts/locations not existing).
- Who specifically represents each thread on the ground (blocked on factions/NPCs not existing).
- The actual hook-by-hook progression between them.

## Act 3 — The Convergence

Per `Main-Storyline-Candidates.md`: all three Act 2 threads converge on **the buried asset** — the unifying secret that turns out to explain all three (why the quarantine exists, what the custodian system was built to protect, what the frequency-squatting faction already knew about and was exploiting or guarding). The endgame becomes a multi-faction negotiation or race over the asset's fate, with the quarantine authority, the awakened system (if not yet resolved by this point), and whatever remains of the frequency-squatting faction all holding a stake — plus the player's own choice of whether to destroy it, control it, expose it to the world, or use it as leverage to end the quarantine.

**Design option carried over from Inner Tepenia's own Act 3 brainstorm (`Act-3_and_Climax_-_Final_Confrontation_Ideas_and_Possibilities.md`):** that document generated seven distinct climax-scenario shapes for Inner Tepenia's own crisis (a cascading grid failure, a data-corruption singularity, a succession crisis, a resource war, and so on) as alternatives to weigh against each other. Toronto Fell Out doesn't need that exercise repeated from scratch — `Main-Storyline-Candidates.md` already settled on the buried-asset convergence as the strongest structure — but the underlying practice (generate several distinct shapes for how a climax could physically/dramatically play out, then pick or combine) is worth doing once there's enough Act 2 content to know what the convergence actually looks like on the ground.

**Tie to `Storyline/Endings/Main-Endings/Climax_Structure_and_District_Ending_Consequences.md`:** that document's Victory-Condition/Main-Negative/Minor-Negatives template is exactly what should be filled in per faction (the quarantine authority, the awakened system's remnant, the frequency-squatting faction's remnant, and the player's own eventual stance) once those factions have names and stakes. This document's job is to establish that the convergence happens; that one's job is to work out what each side actually wins and loses.

**Still fully open, per `Main-Storyline-Candidates.md`'s own final line:** what the buried asset actually is, who the quarantine authority actually is, what the automated system's original standing orders were, and who the frequency-squatting faction turns out to be. None of these need to be decided to lock in the three-threads-into-convergence structure above, but all four need answers before Act 3 can be written in more than skeleton form.

## What Would Unblock Further Detail

In rough order of leverage:
1. At least one of the four still-open questions above gets an answer (the buried asset's identity is probably the highest-leverage one, since `Main-Storyline-Candidates.md` already notes it explains the other three).
2. Toronto Fell Out designs at least a first district/location, giving Act 2's three threads somewhere physical to live.
3. Toronto Fell Out designs at least one faction per thread (a quarantine-authority representative body, the custodian system's remaining "voice" if any, the frequency-squatting faction itself).

Once any of these land, revisit this document, `Main_Quest_Overview.md`, and `Climax_Structure_and_District_Ending_Consequences.md` together — per the reassessment-checkpoint practice noted in the Overview file.
