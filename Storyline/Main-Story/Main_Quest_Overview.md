## The Established Hook

The player's opening goal is climbing the CN Tower and reactivating its radio transmitter — played straight as the apparent main objective, an iconic setpiece that primes the player to believe they've won. It's a **false victory**: completing it doesn't end the game, it reveals the real objective is something else. Four candidate directions for what that something else is — a quarantine perimeter, an awakened automated defense network, a buried asset under the city, a frequency-squatting faction — plus a combined structure weaving all four together, are fully detailed in `Reference/Main-Storyline-Candidates.md`. That combined structure is the strongest current candidate for how the whole main quest fits together, and is what the Act 2/3 material below is built against.

## Design Notes

**Bittersweet-only, no-perfect-fix framing.** Consistent with `General-Overview-Notes/Design-Philosophy.md`'s **No Good Endings** law: whatever the buried asset turns out to be and whoever ends up with it, the resolution should cost something real. The player can plausibly resolve the quarantine/awakened-system/buried-asset throughline, but resolving it cleanly is not on the table — see `Storyline/Endings/Main-Endings/Climax_Structure_and_District_Ending_Consequences.md`'s own **Bittersweet Constant** section.

**Modular, non-linear structure, where content exists to support it.** Act 2's three threads are explicitly designed to run in parallel rather than in a forced sequence — a player can ignore any of them, double down on one, or engage with all three, per the combined structure's own framing in `Main-Storyline-Candidates.md`. This principle should carry forward once Act 2 has real content to be non-linear *about*.

**Reassessment-checkpoint practice.** Inner Tepenia's own process — periodically writing a dated reassessment memo checking an existing story skeleton against newly-designed content, before revising it — is worth adopting as a practice once Toronto Fell Out has districts/factions to check the skeleton above against. There's nothing to reassess yet (there's no deep district/faction canon this skeleton could be out of step with), so no reassessment memo exists in this pass. When Toronto Fell Out's own districts/factions reach the depth Concordia's had, revisit this overview and `Act_2_and_3_Structure_TBD.md` the same way — a short, dated note on what newly-designed content makes visible that wasn't before, followed by a revision pass if warranted.

## Speedrun & Non-Linearity Potential

Toronto Fell Out's Act 1 structure — a single vertical climb with one clear endpoint (the transmitter) — is inherently more speedrun-friendly than Inner Tepenia's own hub-and-spoke district structure, since there's no "which district first" routing question at all: the entire act is one location. The false-victory design compounds this naturally: an "any%"-style run can treat reaching and reactivating the transmitter as the completion point, while a "100%" or true-ending run has to continue past it into Acts 2 and 3. This mirrors Inner Tepenia's own observation that its main quest was built for heavy sequence-breaking (a dedicated player who knows the optimal path finishing far faster than an exploratory one) — worth preserving as a design value once Act 2/3 content exists to sequence-break *through*, rather than prescribing specific routes now that there's no map to route across yet.
