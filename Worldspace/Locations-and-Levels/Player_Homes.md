# Player Homes

**Provenance note:** adapted from Inner Tepenia's own Player Homes design. The user has explicitly confirmed **keeping** the 5-tier structure below, including the un-purchasable, un-questable "Idolized Gift" top tier — this is binding design law for Toronto Fell Out, not a leftover to discard. What has been removed is Inner Tepenia's own per-district home write-ups (13 Zodiac-district homes, all Inner Tepenia-specific lore) — Toronto Fell Out has zero designed districts, so there is nothing yet to hang an actual home list on. The tier *structure* and its rules are real; the specific homes are TBD.

## Overview

The player-character can acquire and use player homes across Toronto Fell Out's districts, once those districts exist. There are two categories:

**Standard player homes** — acquired through reputation, resource contribution, or quest completion. Documented (in structure) in this file, to be organized by district once Toronto Fell Out's districts are designed.

**Companion homes** — each romanced companion unlocks their personal home as an additional player home. These are documented in `Game-Mechanics/Core-Mechanics/Companion-System.md` (see its "Romance Reward — Companion Player Homes" section). They exist separately from and in addition to standard homes. Note: `Worldspace/Locations-and-Levels/Romance_Unlocked_Homes.md` was intentionally not rebuilt as a separate document — Inner Tepenia's version was 100% companion-specific content with no distinct reusable structure beyond what `Companion-System.md` already covers.

## Standard Homes — Structure (Binding)

Each district is planned to offer **five tiers** of player home:

| Tier | Acquisition Difficulty | Notes |
|------|----------------------|-------|
| 1 — Starter | Low to moderate | Reputation + modest resources; no quest lock |
| 2 — Mid-tier | Moderate | Regular contribution + faction standing |
| 3 — Established | Moderate to high | Strong loyalty; often quest-locked or quest-upgraded |
| 4 — Prestige | High; heavily quest-gated | Multi-stage questline required; unique rewards |
| 5 — Idolized Gift | Not purchasable or quest-locked | Given outright upon reaching Idolized reputation with the district — a genuine gift, not an achievement to unlock |

Tiers 1–4 are available by **purchase, quest completion, or a combination of both.** Tier 5 is categorically different: it cannot be bought, bargained for, or earned through any single quest — it's bestowed by the district's own people once the player's reputation reaches **Idolized** status (see `Game-Mechanics/Core-Mechanics/Reputation-System.md` — the top pure-Fame tier on Toronto Fell Out's two-axis Fame/Infamy reputation grid, carried over near-verbatim from Fallout: New Vegas), and each one should be written to feel like a community's own act of gratitude rather than another trial to clear.

### The Idolized Gift Tier — Binding Design Law

- **Never purchasable, never quest-gated.** No amount of currency and no specific quest completion should ever be able to unlock this tier directly — reaching Idolized reputation with the district is the *only* condition.
- **Should read as the community's own initiative, not the player's achievement.** The framing device that worked well in Inner Tepenia's version (worth carrying forward as a writing convention, not specific content): the gift is described as something the community *decided to do*, often without the player being consulted or even present for the decision — the player simply discovers it's happened.
- **Should be tonally distinct from the Tier 4 Prestige home**, not just a bigger/better version of it. Tier 4 typically rewards singular achievement or grinding; Tier 5 should reward *belonging* — a different emotional register entirely (being trusted, being adopted, being quietly exempted from suspicion, etc.), specific to what that district's culture would actually consider the highest form of acceptance.
- **Directly cross-references the Reputation System's own tier logic** — see `Reputation-System.md`'s note under "Named Tiers, Grouped" flagging Idolized (and the related extreme case, Wild Child) as strong candidates for dedicated late-game content. This home tier is one concrete expression of that same design instinct.
